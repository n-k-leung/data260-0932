Incident Response Playbook

Purpose. This playbook is used when a vulnerability in an open source package is being attacked in the
real world, also called actively exploited.

Detection. Alerts come from the dependency scanner, from security mailing lists, or from a customer
report. Anyone who sees an alert must tell the security team within 1 hour.

Emergency deadline. When a Critical vulnerability is actively exploited, the affected service must be
patched or protected with a mitigation within 24 hours. This deadline replaces the normal fix deadline
from the dependency update policy.

Roles. The security team names one incident commander. The incident commander decides on the mitigation,
writes the status updates, and closes the incident after the fix is deployed.

Mitigations. Examples of mitigations are turning off the affected feature, adding a firewall rule, or
pinning the package to a safe older version.

After the incident. Within 5 business days the team holds a review meeting and writes down what went
well and what must change.

Communication. Status updates are posted in the incident channel every 4 hours until the incident is
closed. Customers are informed by the support team only after the incident commander approves the
message.

Evidence. Keep logs, package versions and the time of every action. Do not delete the logs of the
affected service, because they are needed for the review meeting.

Tools. The team uses the dependency scanner, the deployment dashboard and the incident channel during an
incident.
